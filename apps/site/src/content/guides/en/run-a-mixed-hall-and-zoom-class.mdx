Many teachers now teach the same lesson to two groups at once: students in the hall, and students at home on Zoom. Done well, both groups get a good class. Done badly, the online group hears echo, cannot see the board and gives up.

This guide covers the setup that works for both: camera, microphone, seating and a simple routine for questions.

## Teach to the hall, include the online group

You cannot look at two audiences at once. Teach naturally to the students in front of you, and bring the online group in at fixed moments.

- Open the class by greeting both groups.
- Stop every fifteen or twenty minutes to take questions from Zoom.
- Repeat every question from the hall aloud before you answer it, so the online group knows what was asked.

## Camera: show the board, not the room

Online students need to see what you write. They do not need to see the hall.

- **Point the camera at the board,** from the back or side of the hall, high enough that heads do not block the view.
- **Use a tripod or a fixed stand.** A laptop camera on a desk is usually too low and too wide.
- **A phone can work as the camera.** Join it to the meeting as a second device with its microphone and speaker turned off.
- **Check the board on a phone screen.** Write larger than usual, use a thick dark marker, and watch for glare from lights or windows.

If you teach from slides, share your screen from the laptop instead. The board camera is then only for the parts you write by hand.

Point the camera away from students where you can. If students may appear on camera or in recordings, tell them and their parents in advance.

## Microphone: the most important part

Online students will forgive a blurry picture. They will not stay with sound they cannot hear.

- **Wear a microphone.** A wireless or wired clip-on microphone connected to the laptop keeps your voice clear wherever you stand.
- **Use only one microphone in the room for Zoom.** If two devices in the hall have their microphones on, you will get echo and feedback.
- **Keep the hall speaker system separate** from the laptop, or you will send the echo of the hall into Zoom.
- **Turn the laptop speakers down** unless you are taking an online question. You can use one earphone to listen to the online group.

Before every class, do a short sound check with one online student or a helper at home.

## Seating and the hall

A few small changes to the hall make a big difference.

- Keep a clear line between the camera and the board. Leave the aisle in front of the camera empty.
- Put the laptop where you can see the chat without turning your back to the hall.
- Seat students who ask a lot of questions near you, so you can repeat their questions easily.
- Leave a power socket near the laptop and bring an extension cable.

## Questions from the online group

The online group needs a way to ask that does not interrupt the flow.

- Ask them to type questions in the chat, with their name.
- If you have a helper or assistant, they watch the chat and tell you when there is a question.
- At each question break, read the online questions aloud, then answer.

Without a helper, set a fixed routine: check the chat at the end of each section, every time. Students will wait if they know you will come back to them.

## Notes and materials

Both groups should work from the same material.

- Share the notes (tutes) or worksheet before class, as a PDF for the online group and on paper for the hall.
- Use the same page numbers when you refer to them.
- For paper discussions, share the paper in advance so online students can print it or open it on a second screen.

## Attendance for both groups

Keep one attendance list for the class, not two separate systems.

- Mark hall students as they come in.
- For online students, check the participant list against your paid list during the class.
- Combine both into the same register, so you can see each student's attendance whether they came to the hall or joined from home.

If you already use QR or card attendance at the hall, add online joins to the same record. [ReMix](/demo) does this automatically, with hall and Zoom attendance in one register.

## Recording the class

Record the Zoom meeting from the laptop. The recording then has your voice from the microphone and the board from the camera, which is what students need when they revise.

Share the recording only with students who have paid for the month, both hall and online.

## Internet at the hall

Hall internet can be unreliable. Plan for it.

- Test the hall connection at the time of day you teach, not only in the morning.
- Keep a mobile hotspot on a second network ready.
- If the connection drops, keep teaching the hall. Tell online students they will get the recording.

## Rehearsal checklist before your first mixed class

- Camera on a stand, pointed at the board, checked on a phone screen.
- One microphone connected to the laptop, and only one device in the hall with its microphone on.
- Hall speakers kept separate from the laptop.
- Chat visible from where you teach.
- Notes shared with both groups.
- Recording tested.
- Mobile hotspot ready.

Run one short practice class with a helper at home before the real one. Most problems show up in the first five minutes, and it is much better to find them without a full class watching.
