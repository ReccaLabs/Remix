Chasing fees is the part of teaching nobody enjoys. Messages in the group, reminders to parents, slips that do not match, and students who say they paid last week. It takes hours and it spoils the relationship with your class.

The fix is not to chase harder. It is to set up a routine that does the chasing for you, and to apply it the same way to everyone.

## Why chasing does not work

When fee collection depends on you remembering and asking, three things happen:

- **You ask at different times,** so students learn that paying late has no effect.
- **You ask different people differently,** which feels unfair to those who paid on time.
- **You ask in public,** which embarrasses students and makes parents defensive.

A routine fixes all three. The same rule, the same day, the same message, for everyone.

## Step 1: Set one due date and a grace period

Pick one due date for the whole class, for example the 5th of every month. Then decide how many days of grace you give, such as five days.

Keep it simple. Different due dates for different students are hard to track and easy to argue about.

## Step 2: Put the fee rules in writing

Send the rules once, at the start of the year or when a student joins. Pin them in your announcement group.

> Class fees are due on the 5th of each month. Please pay by bank transfer and send the slip, or pay in cash at the hall. Put your index number in the transfer reference. Recordings and notes for the month open once your fee is received. If you are having difficulty paying, please message me privately.

Now when a question comes up, you point to the rule instead of starting a new argument.

## Step 3: Make paying easy

The easier it is to pay, the fewer reminders you need.

- **Use one bank account in the class or institute's name.** Avoid personal or shared family accounts. They make records messy and cause confusion when someone else checks the balance.
- **Ask for the index number in the reference.** Matching a slip to a student becomes a quick search.
- **Accept cash at the hall** if you teach there, and give a receipt every time.
- **Offer card or online payment** if you can. Some families find it easier than going to the bank.

## Step 4: Keep one register

All payments go into one place. A spreadsheet is enough to start:

- **One row per student,** with their index number, name, batch and a parent's phone number.
- **One column per month,** marked Paid, Slip waiting or Due.
- **A notes column** for anything agreed privately, such as a short extension.

Update it the same day a slip arrives. When a parent asks, you can answer in seconds, and you never rely on memory.

Keep the slips too. Save them in a folder by month, named with the index number.

## Step 5: Send reminders on a schedule

Reminders work when they are predictable and polite. Use the same three messages every month:

1. **A few days before the due date:** "Fees for October are due on the 5th."
2. **On the due date:** "Today is the fee day for October. Thank you to everyone who has paid."
3. **After the grace period, privately:** "We have not received your October fee yet. Please send the slip or message me if there is a problem."

The first two go to the whole group and name nobody. Only the last one is personal, and it is sent privately. For younger students, send it to the parent instead.

## Step 6: Lock lessons, not people

When the grace period ends, the fairest thing is for access to follow payment. Recordings, notes and the join link for the new month open when the fee is received, and stay closed until then.

This removes you from the argument. You are not refusing a student. The rule is simply working the way everyone was told it would.

Never name unpaid students in the group, and never stop a student in front of the class. It damages trust with the whole batch, not only that family.

## Handle hardship quietly

Some families go through hard months. Decide in advance how you handle it: a short extension, a reduced fee, or paying in two parts. Agree it privately, write it in your register, and do not discuss it with anyone else.

A clear rule with room for real difficulty is respected. A rule that bends for whoever complains loudest is not.

## Your monthly fee routine

- Before the due date, send the group reminder.
- On the due date, send the thank-you message.
- Every day, update the register when slips arrive.
- After the grace period, send private reminders.
- Open next month's recordings and notes only for paid students.
- At the end of the month, check that the register matches your bank statement.

It takes a little time to set up, and then far less time every month after.
