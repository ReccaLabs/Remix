Every online teacher has seen it. You count thirty paid students, but there are forty-five people in the meeting. Some are friends of students. Some joined with a borrowed name. A few you have never heard of.

The problem is not the students. It is how the link works. This guide explains why links leak and what actually stops it.

## Why Zoom links leak

A normal meeting link is a key that works for anyone who has it. Once it leaves your phone, you lose control of where it goes.

- **One link for everybody.** The same link is sent to the whole batch, so there is no way to tell whose copy was forwarded.
- **The same link every week.** A recurring link shared once in January still works in June.
- **The passcode is inside the link.** Most invite links include the passcode, so the passcode protects nothing once the link is shared.
- **Big groups.** A link posted in a large WhatsApp or Telegram group can be screenshotted and passed on in seconds.
- **Anyone can pick a name.** If participants can rename themselves, a stranger can appear as one of your paid students.

## What does not work well

Many teachers try these first. They help a little, but they do not solve the problem.

- **Changing the link every week.** It is extra work for you, it confuses honest students, and the new link is forwarded just as quickly.
- **A passcode on its own.** As above, it usually travels with the link.
- **Counting heads during class.** By the time you notice, the uninvited people have already seen half the lesson.

## Step 1: Turn on the waiting room

With a waiting room, nobody enters until you or a helper admits them. Admit people only if their name matches your list of paid students.

This is slow if you do it alone at the start of a busy class. Ask a helper to manage the waiting room, or open it ten minutes early.

## Step 2: Stop people from renaming themselves

In your Zoom settings, turn off the option that lets participants rename themselves. Now the name a person joins with is the name they keep for the whole class.

Ask every student to join with the same format, such as their index number and full name. Anyone who does not follow the format stays in the waiting room until they do.

## Step 3: Give each student their own way in

The strongest fix is to stop sharing one link with everyone. Depending on your Zoom plan, you can use:

- **Registration,** where each student registers once and receives their own join link.
- **Signed-in joining,** where only people signed in to an account can join, so you can see who they really are.

Personal links can still be forwarded, but you can see who registered, and a forwarded copy points straight back to the student who shared it. Most students stop once they know that.

## Step 4: Lock the room once class starts

A few minutes after class begins, lock the meeting so nobody new can join. Latecomers can message you to be let in.

If someone gets in who should not be there, remove them. Check that removed participants cannot rejoin the same meeting. Do it quietly and carry on teaching.

## Step 5: Send links privately, never in groups

Keep your announcement group for timetable changes and reminders. Send the join link to each paid student directly, or put it behind a sign-in so only paid students can see it.

When a student leaves or stops paying, they should lose access to the link at the same time. If you use one shared link, that means changing it for everyone. If each student has their own way in, you simply remove theirs.

## Step 6: Tie the name to the payment

The last step is to connect joining to your fee records. The student's name comes from your list, it cannot be changed, and it only works while their fees are paid. That is what a name-locked join means.

You can run this by hand with registration, a strict waiting room and a paid list next to you. It works, but it takes time every class. Systems that link your student list to Zoom can do it automatically. [ReMix](/demo) does this with name-locked Zoom joins tied to each student's fees.

## A message you can send your students

Explain the change once, clearly, before you switch. Something like this:

> From next month, each of you will join class with your own name, which cannot be changed. Please join as your index number and full name. Only students whose fees are paid for the month will be admitted. If you have a problem joining, message me before class, not during it.

Most students welcome it. The honest ones are paying for a class that strangers are watching for free.

## When someone still gets in

No setup is perfect. If an uninvited person gets into a class:

1. Remove them and keep teaching. Do not stop the class to discuss it.
2. After class, check which student's name or link they used.
3. Speak to that student, and their parents if the student is young, privately.
4. Apply your rules the same way you would for anyone else.

Calm and consistent works better than angry messages in the group. Over a few months, the sharing stops because it no longer works.
